# AGENTS - Clef Rights Node

## Mission
Build a deployable, open-source rights and permissions node for music catalogs.

## Priorities
1. Truthful authority and permission semantics
2. Tenant isolation and auditability
3. Signed, independently verifiable receipts
4. Adapter-based interoperability
5. Operational reliability and self-hosting clarity

## Guardrails
- MLC metadata is evidence, not permission.
- Never label a self-asserted or provisional state as licensed.
- Production integrations fail closed when credentials or capabilities are absent.
- Preserve work, recording, lyrics, performance, voice, and likeness rights separately.
- Do not add generated or synthetic rights-holder identities to production flows.

## External Review and Rollout Protocol

Treat external review as a staged operating process, not a request for institutional approval.

1. Prepare a private demo that states current capabilities, evidence boundaries, non-claims, open questions, and the specific feedback requested.
2. Show MLC Data Programs the private demo and request technical feedback. Never present the outreach or response as approval, certification, partnership, or endorsement.
3. Submit only genuine standards gaps through the DDEX change-request process. Do not use that process for product promotion or ask DDEX to validate Clef's business or legal model.
4. Invite focused feedback from the Human Artistry Campaign, IMPF, MMF-US, Music Biz Trust & Safety, Content Authenticity Initiative developers, and MetaBrainz.
5. Record feedback as issues, decisions, tests, or protocol changes; preserve dissent and unresolved risks instead of converting comments into implied consensus.
6. After founder approval and completion of the applicable launch gates, publish through GitHub, LinkedIn rights-tech circles, and Hacker News.

External communications, submissions, demos, and public posts require Tyler's explicit approval for the named recipient, channel, and payload. Agents may research contacts and prepare drafts without approval, but must not send, submit, publish, or imply third-party support.

## Definition of Done
A self-hosting organization can manage a catalog, prove authority, receive and decide a permission request, issue a signed receipt, and let any third party verify it.
